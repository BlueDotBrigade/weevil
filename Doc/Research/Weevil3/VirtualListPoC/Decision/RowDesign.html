#1014 · Weevil 3.0 · for approval

How the new grid handles 100 million rows

The grid on screen only ever shows about 30 rows. The new design makes Weevil keep only those 30 rows as objects, and remember the selection as a few numbers instead of a list of every selected row.

Today

Weevil 2.12 keeps an object for every record

WPF already draws only the rows on screen. But behind the grid, every record is an object in memory, and pressing Ctrl+A copies every one of them into WPF's list of selected rows.

With 100 million records, the selected list alone would hold 100 million entries. After every filter, Weevil 2.12 then selects them again one by one.

The new design

Make a row only when WPF needs it

The records live in a compact table. A filter's results are just record numbers. When WPF asks the grid for "row 27,500,013", the row list makes a small row object for that one record, and lets it go when the row scrolls away.

A row shows its record's content, and reads and writes its comment and pin in a store kept by line number. It asks the selection whether it should look selected.

Selection

Remember the selection as ranges

Instead of a list of every selected row, Weevil stores where each selected stretch of rows starts and ends. Weevil handles the clicks and keys that change the selection itself, rather than leaving them to WPF.

Ctrl+A on 100 million rows stores one range. After a filter, the records still shown stay selected, worked out on a background thread.

Trade-offs

What it costs

  • Weevil owns the selection keys. Click, Shift, Ctrl, the arrow keys, Page Up and Down, Home, End and Ctrl+A are handled by Weevil's code instead of WPF's. The proof of concept and your checks show they behave as before.
  • Copy stops at 100,000 records and points to Export, as already decided for #990.
  • One unusual case costs more: after a filter that showed every 10th row, removing the filter splits a selection into 10 million small ranges: 0.3 s and about 80 MB.
  • Each filter still pauses the window about a quarter of a second while WPF rebuilds the rows on screen. That happens with any design, and in Weevil today.

The decision

Approve this design for #1015: a row object only for the rows on screen, and the selection kept as ranges, with Weevil handling the selection keys and clicks itself.
